# MHM Global — brand direction

Original identity study for a homepage prototype. This is not a trademark search and not a claim of clearance, registration, or exclusive right.

## Direction

MHM Global distributes and sells low-voltage technology for homes, businesses, and buildings, at retail and wholesale. The company is not an installer, manufacturer, or security installation company.

The identity should feel light, modern, precise, and architectural: white and mist fields, ink navy, one restrained blue. It should be credible on an invoice, a carton, and a business card, and memorable as a distributor rather than as a security contractor or a gadget brand.

Visual idea: connected volumes. Piers, datums, floor plates, and joints — not globes, circuit traces, shields, or stock photography.

## Palette

| Role | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Page ground, cards |
| Mist | `#F7F9FC` | Alternate sections, drawing sheets |
| Ink | `#142B44` | Text, structure, primary buttons |
| Blue | `#1688CE` | Joints, focus, drawing lines, large accents |
| Line | `#DCE8F1` | Hairlines, quiet fills |

Blue on white is about 3.9:1. It is used for graphics, focus rings, and large display moments. Body text, small labels, and links stay ink navy (`#142B44`, about 14:1 on white). Muted copy uses `#3E5670`.

## Typography

- **Syne** for headlines. Expressive, slightly architectural, not a default corporate gothic.
- **Outfit** for the wordmark, navigation, and reading text. Neutral and durable at small sizes.

Wordmark: `MHM` in semibold, `GLOBAL` in small tracked capitals beneath. The symbol can stand alone on a favicon or stamp.

## Logo studies

Five original marks. All are orthogonal or sectional drawings about structure and connection.

1. **Datum — selected.** Three piers crossed by a datum line. The center pier splits so a circular joint stays visible in pure monochrome. Reads as colonnade plus connection.
2. **Plan.** A floor plate with an asymmetric corridor. Elegant, softer at 16px.
3. **Portal.** Two offset frames and a threshold. About entry and depth, less immediate as a stamp.
4. **Bay.** A three-bay facade and a transom node. Clearly architectural, busier when tiny.
5. **Section.** Two roof pitches sharing a valley. The most literal M, and the first to fill in at invoice size.

## Why Datum

- At 16px it is still three strokes, a bar, and a dot.
- One ink color is enough for shipping labels, blind-emboss, and a navy footer. The joint does not depend on a second color to be seen.
- The geometry is the same idea as the homepage: separate volumes, one connecting line, a single node.
- Plan, Portal, Bay, and Section each do one of those jobs. Datum does both without turning into a picture of a building.

Color lockup: navy piers, blue datum and joint. Monochrome lockup: `currentColor`, for print and for reversal out of navy.

## Homepage structure

1. Sticky navigation — logo, Home, About, Product Categories, Wholesale, Contact, Business Inquiries.
2. Hero — “Technology for Every Space.” A measured axonometric of a home volume, a glazed link, and a taller building, with five numbered supply points.
3. Five categories — Surveillance & Security, Networking & Connectivity, Smart Home Technology, Access Control, Life Safety.
4. About — distributor and seller, retail and wholesale, explicitly not an installer or manufacturer.
5. Wholesale — “Your Business. Better Connected.” An elevation drawing of homes, businesses, and buildings on one datum. No pricing program, minimum, or stock claim.
6. Contact — “Let’s Build a Connection.” Demo only; nothing is sent or stored.
7. Footer — navigation, categories, contact lines marked to be confirmed.

## Hero visual

A line-drawn axonometric on a pale sheet, not a render and not a photograph. Lit window panes and a blue route between nodes show low-voltage supply running through otherwise ordinary architecture. Numbered points open a short readout and can jump to the category or the inquiry form.

## Voice

Plain and specific. No invented history, metrics, certifications, testimonials, coverage maps, or staff. Unpublished contact details stay “To be confirmed.” Retail channels such as Amazon may be named; no storefront URL is invented.
